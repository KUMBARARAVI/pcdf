About the Project

What is the AWD Project?

The Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) Project is a sustainable agriculture initiative led by Progressive Community & Development Foundation (PCDF) Trust, designed to help paddy farmers reduce water consumption while maintaining or improving crop productivity.

AWD is a globally recognized technique where farmers periodically allow the water level in paddy fields to fall — even below the soil surface — before re-irrigating. This simple, low-cost approach can save up to 30% of irrigation water without reducing yield.

PCDF Trust works directly with farming communities through field visits, training sessions, AWD tube installation, water-level monitoring, and continuous technical support. Every activity is documented to build an evidence base for the project's real-world impact.

The Technique

How Alternate Wetting & Drying Works

AWD replaces continuous flooding with a simple cycle of controlled drying and re-irrigation — monitored using an inexpensive perforated tube.

1
🔧

Install the AWD Tube

A perforated PVC tube is inserted ~30 cm deep into the paddy field. It acts as a water-level observation well.

2
👁️

Monitor Water Level

Farmers look inside the tube every 2–3 days to check whether water is visible above or below the soil surface.

3
⏳

Allow Controlled Drying

Water is allowed to drop 15–20 cm below the soil surface before re-irrigation, except during critical growth stages.

4
💧

Re-Irrigate at the Right Time

Once the water level drops to the threshold, farmers re-irrigate the field with a shallow 5 cm layer of water.

5
🔁

Repeat the Cycle

The wet–dry cycle is repeated throughout the growing season, except during flowering and panicle initiation stages.

6
📝

Record & Report

Field staff record water level readings, field conditions, and farmer observations for ongoing project analysis.

Why Does AWD Work?

Conventional paddy farming keeps fields flooded throughout the season, consuming 2–3× more water than needed and producing large amounts of methane — a potent greenhouse gas.

AWD mimics the natural wet–dry cycle that paddy roots can tolerate. During dry phases, soil oxygen increases, roots strengthen, and anaerobic methane production is suppressed — all without harming the crop.

What We Do

Key Field Activities

PCDF Trust field teams carry out five core activities to ensure AWD is implemented correctly and farmers receive continuous support.

📚

Farmer Training

Farmers are introduced to AWD techniques, water management principles, and the practical benefits of controlled irrigation through hands-on field sessions and community workshops.

🎓 Capacity Building
🔧

AWD Tube Installation

Perforated PVC tubes are installed by trained field staff in selected paddy plots. Each tube allows farmers to directly monitor underground water levels without specialized equipment.

🛠️ Infrastructure
🔍

Field Monitoring

Regular field visits observe water levels, crop growth stages, soil conditions, and actual farming practices. Issues are identified early and addressed immediately.

📡 Monitoring
📋

Data Collection

Field information — location, date, water level readings, crop condition, and farmer participation — is systematically recorded to support monitoring, evaluation, and reporting.

📊 Data & Evidence
🤝

Continuous Farmer Support

Field officers provide ongoing technical guidance throughout the entire cultivation cycle — from land preparation to harvest — ensuring farmers can confidently apply AWD independently.

🫂 Technical Support

💧

Water Conservation

Farmers save an average of 30% irrigation water per season — reducing strain on local water sources and lowering pumping costs.

🌾

Sustained Yield

Safe-AWD implementation maintains crop yields equivalent to continuous flooding, with some farmers reporting marginal yield improvements.

💰

Improved Livelihoods

Reduced irrigation costs directly increase net farm income. Farmers report saving ₹1,500–₹3,000 per hectare per season in pump electricity.

🌍

Climate Benefits

Reduced methane emissions from periodically drained fields contribute to global climate change mitigation goals under sustainable agriculture.

📊

Evidence-Based Agriculture

Structured data collection builds a replicable evidence base that can inform regional policy, scaling, and future research.

👨‍🌾

Farmer Empowerment

Farmers gain knowledge, confidence, and independent capability to apply AWD — creating a lasting legacy beyond the project period.
